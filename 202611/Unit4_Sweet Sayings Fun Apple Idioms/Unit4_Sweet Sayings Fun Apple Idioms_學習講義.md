## 二、課文與翻譯

### ¶1 引言
What do you think of when you hear the word *apple*? Most people probably **picture** the sweet, **crunchy** fruit first. But did you know that apples also appear in many fun English expressions? One famous example is the Big Apple, a **nickname** for New York City. Although this nickname is quite old, it **caught on** in the 1970s **because of** a **campaign** to attract tourists. Let's look at some other well-known apple **idioms**.

> 你聽到 apple 這個字時，會想到什麼？大多數人可能會先想到那種香甜爽脆的水果。但你知道蘋果也出現在許多有趣的英文說法裡嗎？一個著名的例子是 the Big Apple，也就是紐約市的暱稱。雖然這個暱稱由來已久，但它是在 1970 年代因為一項吸引觀光客的宣傳活動才流行起來。我們來看看其他一些廣為人知的蘋果慣用語吧。

### ¶2 The Apple of One's Eye（心肝寶貝）
If someone is the apple of your eye, that person is very special to you. For example, a mother might call her son the apple of her eye **because** she loves him more than anyone else in the world.

> 如果某人是你的「眼中的蘋果」，那個人對你來說非常特別。舉例來說，一位母親可能會說兒子是她的心肝寶貝，因為她愛他勝過世界上的任何人。

### ¶3 An Apple a Day Keeps the Doctor Away（一天一蘋果，醫生遠離我）
This is a very common **saying** about health. It means that **the more** fresh, healthy food you eat, **the stronger** your body may become. Of course, eating just one apple every day doesn't **guarantee** that you'll never **come down with** an illness. But if you eat well, you might not need to see a doctor as often.

> 這是一句關於健康、非常常見的俗諺。它的意思是，你吃越多新鮮健康的食物，身體就可能變得越強壯。當然，每天只吃一顆蘋果並不能保證你永遠不會生病。但如果你吃得好，或許就不需要那麼常去看醫生。

### ¶4 Comparing Apples and Oranges（拿蘋果跟柳橙比）
Sometimes people compare two things that seem similar but are actually completely different. This is called comparing apples and oranges. Think of a smartphone and a laptop. The two **devices** serve different purposes, so they are hard to judge by the same standard.

> 有時候，人們會拿兩樣看似相似、實際上卻完全不同的東西來比較。這就叫做「拿蘋果跟柳橙比」。想想智慧型手機和筆記型電腦。這兩種裝置的用途不同，所以很難用同一個標準來評判。

### ¶5 A Bad Apple（害群之馬）
A bad apple is a person in a group who **stirs up** trouble for everyone else. The idiom comes from a **barrel** of apples. If one apple goes bad, it can **spoil** all the good ones around it.

> 「壞蘋果」是指團體中替其他所有人惹麻煩的人。這個慣用語源自一桶蘋果。如果有一顆蘋果壞掉，它可能會讓周圍所有的好蘋果都跟著變質。

### ¶6 How Do You Like Them Apples?（怎麼樣，服了吧？）
This **playful** question is another way to say, "What do you think about that?" People often use it after they prove they are right, win an argument, or succeed at something. They **come across as** proud, and they may even be **teasing** someone who doubted them.

> 這個帶點調侃意味的問句，是「你覺得怎麼樣？」的另一種說法。人們常在證明自己是對的、贏了一場爭論，或在某件事上成功之後這麼說。他們給人一種得意的感覺，甚至可能是在取笑曾經懷疑他們的人。

### ¶7 結語
English speakers **have been using** apple idioms to describe love, health, differences, trouble, and pride. That is **a wide range of** meanings for one small fruit. With this many expressions to choose from, you can probably find one that fits almost any situation. How do you like them apples?

> 說英語的人一直都在用蘋果慣用語來形容愛、健康、差異、麻煩和驕傲。對一種小小的水果來說，這真是相當廣泛的意義。有這麼多說法可以挑，你大概能找到一個適用於幾乎任何情況的說法。怎麼樣，服了吧？

## 四、Language Notes

### 1. catch on
- (a) 流行起來（不及物，主詞多為事物）：*The dance caught on after people saw it online.*
- (b) 理解、明白（＋to sth）：*It took Keira some time to catch on to the new system.*

### 2. because of vs. because
- because of ＋ N／V-ing：*The boy cried because of the pain in his leg.*
- because ＋ 子句：*Chase was excited because he got a new bike.*

### 3. 雙重比較級
`the + 比較級 + S + V, the + 比較級 + S + V`
- *The narrower the road becomes, the more carefully you should drive.*
- *The fewer mistakes you make, the more points you get.*
- 省略：*The more the merrier.*／*The cleaner your room (is), the easier it is to find things in it.*

### 4. stir up
- (a) 挑起、煽動（壞事）：*Isabelle's post stirred up an argument between the two groups.*
- (b) 激起情緒：*The story stirred up excitement in the children.*

### 5. come across
- (a) come across as ＋ N／Adj. 給人……的印象：*The new coach comes across as a kind person.*
- (b) 及物：偶然發現：*Brody came across an old photo in his room.*

### 6. 現在完成進行式 have/has been V-ing
- 搭配 for／since／recently／lately：*Mia has been learning French since last year.*
- 比較：*Brian has lived / has been living in Hualien for three years.*（完成式重結果，完成進行式重過程）
