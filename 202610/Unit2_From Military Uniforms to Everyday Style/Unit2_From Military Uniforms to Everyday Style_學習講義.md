## 課文

**¶1** You have probably *come across* clothes that remind you of the **military** while shopping. This is because many well-known fashion items began as functional uniforms for soldiers, sailors, and **pilots**. These clothes needed to be strong, comfortable, and practical. Today, people wear them simply because they *look good*. Let's look at four common examples.

**¶2** The bomber jacket, or flight jacket, was first made for airplane pilots when cockpits were open and very cold. Early versions appeared during World War I, and around 1950 the US Air Force **introduced** the MA-1 jacket, which was made from nylon. This material kept pilots warm and dry. Over time, the flight jacket became a popular everyday item for both men and women.

**¶3** Similarly, aviator sunglasses were **originally** made to help pilots. In the 1930s, pilots struggled with the bright sun. The American company Bausch & Lomb developed these special glasses to cover the whole eye area and **protect** against strong light. They called this design Ray-Ban, which is now perhaps the most famous sunglasses brand in the world.

**¶4** It may sound surprising, but the modern T-shirt also has its origins in the military. From 1913, the US **Navy** started giving sailors short-**sleeved** white shirts to wear under their heavy uniforms. Working inside ships was very hot, so sailors often took off their uniform jackets and just wore the T-shirts. Later, actors such as Marlon Brando wore them in films, and this *made them very fashionable*.

**¶5** Cargo pants are another well-known example. The British and US armies were among the first to use them. Soldiers needed the **extra** pockets to carry various items while keeping their hands free. In the 1990s, skateboarders and young people started wearing them because they were **loose** and comfortable.

**¶6** As you can see, military history has had a **significant** influence on modern fashion. Some of the world's most recognizable everyday styles *started out as* functional military gear. *The next time you wear* one of these pieces, remember that even items that were created for practical purposes may become fashion one day.
