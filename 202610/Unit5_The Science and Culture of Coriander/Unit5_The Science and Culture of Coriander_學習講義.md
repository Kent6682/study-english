## 🎬 一、四格圖解大意 (4-Panel Storyboard)

1. **起（DNA 與肥皂味之謎）**：香菜是全球最具爭議的食材！討厭香菜並非挑食，而是因為體內擁有特定基因（`specific gene`），對香菜與肥皂共有的化學物質「醛類（`aldehydes`）」特別敏感，大腦因而將其解讀（`process`）為肥皂臭味。
2. **承（美英稱呼差異與臭蟲字源）**：美式英文把香菜葉叫 `cilantro`、種子叫 `coriander`；英澳則統稱 `coriander`。更有趣的是，`coriander` 源自古希臘文的「臭蟲（`stink bug`）」，證明古今中外對它的味道爭論不休！
3. **轉（跨越八千年的香料絲路）**：香菜種子最早可追溯至西元前 6000 年的黎凡特（`the Levant`）洞穴。羅馬人用它調味麵包與保存肉類，中世紀傳遍歐洲，再隨探險家（`explorers`）飄洋過海成為中美洲與亞洲料理的靈魂。
4. **合（台灣領軍的現代香菜狂潮）**：當代食品大廠掀起獵奇香菜風潮！台灣推出香菜奶茶、洋芋片與冰淇淋；日本必勝客推出香菜披薩大受歡迎（`such a hit that...`），甚至加碼「雙倍香菜量（`twice the amount of`）」回歸！

## 📖 二、逐段中英對照與重點標註

### 【Day 12】
- **Para 1**: Coriander has one of the world’s most hotly **debated** **flavors**. Many people love the herb’s fresh `taste` and enjoy it in **cuisines** from many cultures. However, many others hate it and claim it `tastes like` soap. Yet this **dispute** is about more than personal **preference**—the reason for it, in fact, `lies in` our DNA.
- **Para 2**: Some people have a **specific** **gene** that makes them especially `sensitive to` chemicals that are called aldehydes. Because coriander contains these chemicals, which are also found in soap, some people’s brains **process** the herb as unpleasant. Interestingly, this **reaction** is `far less common` among people who grew up eating coriander regularly.
- **Para 3**: Even the plant’s name comes with some curious facts. In American English, the leaves are called cilantro, `while` the seeds are called coriander. In places such as the UK and Australia, both parts are simply called coriander. The word “coriander” comes from a Greek word for “stink bug” because the ancient Greeks thought fresh coriander smelled like one. Clearly, the **debate** over this herb is nothing new!

### 【Day 13】
- **Para 4**: Coriander’s story also **stretches** back thousands of years. The oldest known coriander seeds were found in caves in the Levant and `date back to` roughly 6000 BCE. The Romans used them to **flavor** bread and **preserve** meat, and by the Middle Ages, the plants `had spread` across Europe. From there, **explorers** carried the herb to new lands until it found a home in Central American and Asian **cuisines**.
- **Para 5**: Today, food companies are creating unusual coriander **products**, and Taiwan is `leading the way`. You can find coriander milk tea, **coriander-flavored** potato chips, and even coriander ice cream. In Japan, Pizza Hut **released** a coriander pizza that became `such a hit` with younger customers `that` the **chain** brought it back with `twice the amount of` the herb.
- **Para 6**: **Devoted** fans even hold special festivals to celebrate the herb, and there are now **perfumes** that smell just like fresh coriander. `Love it or hate it`, coriander has clearly `earned its place` at the table, and `by the looks of it`, that isn’t changing anytime soon.
