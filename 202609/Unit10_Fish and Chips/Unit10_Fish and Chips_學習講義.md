## 讀門秘笈：Skimming

There may be times when you want to **get a sense of** what a text is about without having to read every single word. That's why skimming is an important skill to learn. Skimming is a technique that **allows us to** gain a general understanding of a text, but how do we do it?

1. Look at titles, headings, and subheadings to understand what a text is about
2. Focus on the first and last sentences of paragraphs or sections—these may **sum up** what is being described
3. Pay attention to repeated words or phrases that seem important, such as bold or italicized words and numbered lists
4. Check charts, graphs, or photo captions to understand the main idea

## 課文

**¶1** Most British people think fish and chips is the country's national dish. There are around 10,500 fish-and-chip shops in the UK, and the meals are sold in just about every **pub** and traditional restaurant across the nation. *As such*, British people eat **roughly** 382 million **portions** of fish and chips every year.

**¶2** Records **suggest** that fish and chips was first served in the 1860s, and the earliest known shops were opened in London and Lancashire. The meal soon *became popular with* the working class as fish from the North Sea was *easy to come by*. In fact, fish and chips was *so popular that* it was even **exempted** from rationing during the Second World War, as people believe it improved **morale**.

**¶3** Today, going to the fish-and-chip shop is still a big part of British life. Modern fish-and-chip shops, or "chippies," sell a huge **variety** of foods, *from* pies and sausages *to* pickled eggs and spring rolls. There's *so much* to choose from, so people still love them.
